### Added

- Internal family-fork reservations retain original keys and child IDs through journal compaction, reject conflicting durable owners, and re-establish reservation durability after an unacknowledged write.
- Family registration shares reservation serialization and refuses to register a reserved child as an unrelated root. Live journal observation no longer requires treating its owner as interrupted; ordinary lifecycle recovery remains unchanged.
- Reservations can retain immutable source references, owner generations, complete-snapshot anchors and destination resource bindings. Changed bindings and duplicate resource reservations fail closed; older unbound records cannot gain fabricated provenance. These records do not grant execution or certify child publication.
- The internal ownership coordinator can publish a reserved inactive child's reference and lineage in one family-state replacement after actual teardown, authenticated managed-transcript closure, and source/destination revalidation. Materialized transcript receipts cannot be replaced; failed publication retains uncertainty. The producer must still retain source quiescence, and journal terminal records alone do not establish commit.
- Original-key lookup can confirm the matching committed lineage after a lost reply or later admitted continuation, with fresh file/directory durability checks and no reference, revision, or fence rewrite. Missing or unresolved keys never authorize re-execution; historical commit does not certify current transcript availability or grant runtime admission.
